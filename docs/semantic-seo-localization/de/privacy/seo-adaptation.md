# Target-Market SEO Adaptation — privacy (de)

## Source Topic Summary
- Primary topic: what personal/usage data HeightComparison (heightcomparisoncalculator.com) collects, does not collect, and how it is used/retained.
- Secondary topics: Image to Height upload retention, shareable chart link retention, Google Analytics usage, server logs, user rights (deletion request, GA opt-out), children's privacy, third-party links, policy changes, contact.
- Concrete SEO-relevant terms in the English version: "Privacy Policy", "Google Analytics", "Image to Height", "shareable chart links", "data retention", "opt-out", "children's privacy", "data deletion".

## Target-Market Reasoning (de)
- German searchers overwhelmingly search legal/consumer-facing pages of this kind under **"Datenschutzerklärung"** (lit. "data protection declaration"), not a literal translation of "Privacy Policy" (which would read as "Datenschutzrichtlinie" or "Privatsphäre-Richtlinie" — both far less searched). "Datenschutzerklärung" is the established, near-universal German legal/SEO term for this page type (driven by GDPR/DSGVO usage) and is what a German-language site is expected to title this page.
- "Datenschutz" (data protection/privacy) alone is the head term German users search when concerned about what a tool collects; it is the natural umbrella keyword for this page, not a literal "Privatsphäre" (which reads as "privacy" in an interpersonal/psychological sense, not the data-protection sense).
- "Personenbezogene Daten" ("personal data") is the standard GDPR-driven German phrase for what a privacy policy discusses — used in place of a literal "persönliche Daten", which is understandable but not the term German compliance/SEO content converges on.
- "Datenlöschung" / "Daten löschen" ("data deletion") is the natural German phrasing for the user-rights section, rather than a literal "Datenentfernung".
- Google Analytics, Opt-out, and Cookie remain in English/borrowed form — these are internationally recognized product/technical terms in German tech and legal-page vocabulary, not translated into German equivalents (translating "Cookie" to a German-only term would read as unnatural on a German tech site).
- Google Search Console MCP tools were not used for this dry run — no active property/query data was consulted; this stage is LLM-reasoning-only, per the skill's optional-MCP rule.
